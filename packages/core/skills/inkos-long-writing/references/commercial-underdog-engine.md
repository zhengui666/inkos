# Commercial underdog story engine

Use a causal network, not a fixed plot sequence. Explicit author goals and canon remain authoritative. Do not retrofit existing books just because they lack a structured contract. Infer this design from the supplied brief; do not turn these fields into a user questionnaire.

## Recognizable promise, distinctive hook

In `readerContract`, connect familiar genre pleasure to one new constraint or opportunity that changes decisions. A novel noun or complicated institution alone is not a hook. Title, premise and opening must agree. Do not copy source names, prose, scene order or signature combinations. Market patterns are hypotheses, not audience or revenue guarantees.

Default new commercial work to `commercial-underdog`. Use `author-directed` for an actual alternative instruction or source constraint, named in `authorDirection`, not to evade the default. A chosen literary or tragic story does not need an imposed victory ladder.

## Causal relationships, variable ordering

Define what the protagonist cannot afford, obtain, protect or refuse; what concrete improvement matters; usable capital and its limits; the opposing interest or pressure and its leverage; what the protagonist contributes; and the meaningful return. These are connected design questions, not mandatory scenes or an event order. One event may serve several functions; explanations and gains may arrive in different orders.

Observed opening mechanisms include defending an already-held resource before expanding power; turning unfamiliar potential into usable skill through help and experiments; surviving confinement through observation and cooperation; and making personal experience valuable in a reciprocal alliance. Select or combine a mechanism fitting this brief rather than cloning any benchmark. Limited opening samples do not establish whole-book or short-story formulas.

Systems, rebirth, abilities, hidden identities, skills, information, relationships and chance are valid opportunities. Show how the chosen advantage can be used and what remains difficult. Luck or allies may contribute without replacing all consequential choices. Limits may be access, responsibility, time or exposure; do not force suffering, injury or punishment into every win. A disadvantaged person gaining ability differs from an already-powerful person revealing it; do not silently swap those promises.

A gain may be money, skill, protection, trust, standing or freedom to act. Show why it matters and let its effect land. Routine payment, narrator praise and applause alone may fail the promised scale. Preserve gains into later circumstances. A future counterattack can remain unknown; do not invent one. Cast cards need concrete wants, leverage, relationships to the desired gain and distinct speech, not compulsory sympathetic biographies.

## Chapter delivery

Populate `readerDelivery`: setup/advance/payoff/aftermath, wanted outcome, opening move, resistance, initiative, earned change, felt consequence and carry-forward. Explain deferred rewards and what readers get instead. Make the plan causal and observable; keep labels out of narration. Aftermath need not invent another obstacle. A final chapter or one-shot closes its promised outcome; carry-forward can mean the lasting resolved change, not a sequel hook. Only actual prose establishes state or a paid promise.

## Opening and language

Read roughly the first ten sentences as a newcomer: what is happening, what is wanted or at risk, what concrete contrast or possibility stands out, and why continue? This is an inspection window, not a quota requiring a villain, complete system or win. Intentional mystery about causes differs from confusion about the immediate event. Later chapters start from changed circumstances.

Use direct concrete phrasing, a clear action-response-consequence chain and dialogue driven by different aims. Let confrontation and emotional release be strong when fitting; do not turn them into polite errands or procedure reports. Description and thought should sharpen experience. Avoid lore inventories, unexplained terminology and ornate abstractions that delay understanding. Easy reading does not mean childish diction, endless insults or identical fragments. Paragraphs follow the language and speaker; counts cannot certify style. Repair a weak premise at its source, not with louder insults or cosmetic polishing.
