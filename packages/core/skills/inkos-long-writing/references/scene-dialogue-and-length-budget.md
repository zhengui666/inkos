# Scene, dialogue, and length budget

## Build length before prose

Treat the chapter target as a scene budget, not a request to pad or trim a finished chapter. Choose the number of scenes and assign the available words or characters according to dramatic work and the requested form. A scene earns space through action, resistance, discovery, relationship change, or consequence. Before expanding or cutting, check the work done by emotion, perception, relationships, voice, and rhythm as well as external events. Revise the plan if the space has no such function; do not invent incidents merely to fill it.

For a new Chinese chapter with no different agreed target, plan roughly 2000–2500 non-whitespace body characters including punctuation, excluding the title. This is a chapter budget, not the total length of a short work. Preserve explicit or already saved targets during continuation. English uses its own declared word budget; never silently reinterpret the Chinese numbers as English words.

## Dialogue carries pressure

In a confrontation, each meaningful reply changes leverage, reveals a constraint, creates a debt, or forces a choice. Before cutting an exchange that restates a known position, check whether its recurrence changes emotion, perception, relationship, voice, or rhythm. Preserve meaningful repetition; compress it only when none of these functions is served. Let subtext arise from what a character risks by speaking, withholding, or changing the subject.

Use naturally varied paragraphs in the actual language. If the author requested a Chinese paragraph range, treat it as a preference with meaningful exceptions, not a quality score. Avoid habitual tiny standalone fragments and long bare question-answer chains. Integrate relevant action, expression, environment and narration without assigning a gesture to every line or confusing who speaks. English follows natural paragraphing in its own syntax, without a converted Chinese paragraph quota.

## Repair measured drift

When a draft misses its hard range, repair the scenes responsible for the drift. Expand a missing action, consequence, or reaction beat; compress recap, repeated interiority, and duplicated description. Preserve established facts and the chapter's intended turn. Never rewrite the whole chapter merely to hit a number.

## Voice persists across scenes

Before a consequential exchange, distinguish what each speaker wants, knows, assumes, and risks revealing. Express those differences through the response itself, not only a dialogue tag or dialect. A reserved character may become blunt under pressure, but the change needs a cause in the scene or established arc. Preserve such earned variation instead of normalizing every voice while polishing.

## Revise at the appropriate scale

Compare open creative choices, establish a causal skeleton, draft scenes, review, and make targeted repairs at the scale of the current scene, chapter, or story arc. This is a flexible method inside the active operation, not a mandatory sequence of new tasks. Preserve accepted choices and skip steps that add no value.

For revision, first locate the faulty layer: continuity, scene structure, voice, or phrasing. Repair only the authorized scope, then compare the affected passage with the preceding state and following consequences. A successful prose polish preserves plot facts, viewpoint, character decisions, and unresolved promises. A missing setup may need a separate structural change; it is not permission to invent prior events during polishing.
