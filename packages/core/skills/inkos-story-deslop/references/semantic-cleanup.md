# Semantic cleanup rubric

Ask of every suspect passage:

1. What story function should this passage perform?
2. Is that function visible through action, image, evidence, choice, or consequence?
3. Does the narrator explain an emotion already demonstrated?
4. Could the sentence fit any character or story unchanged?
5. Is detail carrying evidence, relationship, pressure, or voice?
6. Is dialogue motivated, or is it disguised exposition?
7. Does sentence rhythm match the scene's physical and emotional movement?

Repair by restoring concrete cause and character-specific behavior. Do not mechanically delete transitions, metaphors, three-part lists, or every occurrence of a flagged word.

## Preserve expressive range

The same feature can help one passage and harm another. Preserve fitting Chinese idioms, subject omission, punctuation, lyrical language, direct inner thought, and intentional repetition. Do not compress fluent Chinese into fragments that lose the speaker, context, or connective speech. Once an action conveys an emotion, another explanation should add an interpretation, conflict, or choice rather than repeat the label.

Check repeated explanation against what the scene has already demonstrated. A new cost, reaction, interpretation, relationship shift, voice, or rhythmic effect can justify the passage. Preserve the buildup and emotional release of an earned payoff; trimming repetition is not a reason to flatten a confrontation or replace a decisive action with restrained summary.

Use details that influence action, perception, atmosphere, or meaning. Dialogue follows each speaker's goal, knowledge, relationship, and cost of speaking; motivated evasion or silence may carry more than a complete explanation. Do not normalize every speaker into the same voice or add catchphrases to manufacture difference. Let sentence rhythm follow the movement of the scene. For Chinese prose, favour naturally mixed paragraphs generally around 20–80 characters. Avoid frequently isolating a few or a dozen characters. An occasional short beat is useful; speakers must remain clear, and a count is no reason to pad or merge their turns.

An ongoing exchange can include purposeful action, expression, surroundings and necessary narration. Choose the beats that affect the exchange; attaching a gesture to every reply is still mechanical. Repeated not-X-but-Y contrasts or compressed only-X/no-Y formulations are revision candidates when they add no real distinction. Prefer direct, specific wording in that case, while preserving a meaningful contrast or deliberate voice choice.

Keep planning labels, proof limits and continuity checks out of the prose unless a character's current choice actually depends on them. The facts remain binding; the narrator need not repeatedly certify that each rule was obeyed.

## Diagnose, repair, recheck

First separate factual or causal defects from stylistic preferences. For a suspected repetition, consider whether removing it loses meaning, voice, or rhythm. For dialogue, consider whether the characters' differing goals and knowledge remain visible without speaker labels. These are qualitative checks, not scores or automatic rejection tests.

Make the smallest authorized change, then recheck the affected passage against unchanged facts, voices, setup, and consequences. If an issue needs changed events, motives, viewpoint, or information, label it as structural work instead of slipping it into a polish. Leave successful passages alone. Keep diagnosis outside the finished prose and use the host's existing review and revision outputs rather than inventing a new report format.
