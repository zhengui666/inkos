# Long-form market research rubric

## Evidence

- Record source, publication date, observation window, platform, and whether a number is first-party, third-party, or inferred.
- For every chart-based comparison, record chart name, category, ranking metric, active filters, observation time, and official URL. Keep displayed position scoped to that chart and filter state. Do not infer retention, sales or an opening's effect from a ranking metric that does not measure them.
- Separate chart, synopsis, contents and actually read prose evidence. Name the chapters or passages read and the unread scope; label excerpts or incomplete access. An opening sample cannot establish whole-book payoff.
- Distinguish durable reader needs from a temporary chart pattern.
- Compare at least two sources when making a market-level claim.
- State missing evidence rather than filling it with genre common sense.
- Keep relevant counterexamples that challenge a proposed mechanism; state when none were found or accessible. Treat transferable mechanisms as hypotheses to test, not proven causes of popularity.

## Deliverable

1. Market boundary and user objective.
2. Observed signals with sources, chart context and actual reading extent.
3. Audience and reading-context implications.
4. Transferable story mechanisms.
5. Risks, saturation, and counter-signals.
6. Two or three differentiated directions, not a single mandatory formula.

Do not copy titles, character configurations, signature scenes, or wording from a comparable work.
